Select text and choose **Comment** in the toolbar, or press `Cmd+Shift+M` (`Ctrl+Shift+M` on
Windows and Linux), to start a thread. A small 💬 appears after the passage; the panel on the right
lists the page's threads. Reply, mention teammates with `@login`, and resolve a thread when it's
done. Highlighted passages and the 💬 open their thread when you tap them. Authors can edit and
delete their own comments; edited ones are marked, and earlier versions stay in the Git history.
When two people comment on the same page at the same moment, both comments are kept.

Mentioned teammates see it in their [mention inbox](./mentions.mdx), and by email or in team chat if
that is set up.

## Stored in the file

Each thread is a Markdown footnote: a mark after the commented passage and the thread at the end of
the page, one line per comment.

```markdown
The quarterly numbers[^c-7f3a] look good.

[^c-7f3a]: 💬 on "quarterly numbers"

    - **@alice** · 2026-11-14 10:32 — Where do these come from?
    - **@bob** · 2026-11-14 11:02 — From the Q3 controlling export.
```

- **On GitHub** threads show as footnotes: a number at the passage, the discussion at the foot of the
  page, linked both ways.
- **The mark is part of the text.** It moves when you move the sentence, and it stays when you reword
  it, so the thread stays with its passage. If the passage is deleted, the thread stays and says the
  passage is gone.
- **Headings and code** get no mark (GitHub would change the heading's link). Their threads are
  found by the quoted text and show in GitQuire and in the raw file.
- **Resolving** removes the mark and the thread from the file. The Git history keeps both.
- **Diffs** show one line per reply, and replies made on two branches usually merge without
  conflict.

The exact rules, the tolerant reading of hand-written replies and example files are in
[Comment format](./comment-format.mdx). Pages from before this format keep working; they are
converted the next time they are saved, or all at once with `gq comments convert`.

## When files change in a sync

When someone pushes a commit to GitHub and a sync brings it in, GitQuire checks
the comments of the open page: a thread deleted while its mark is still in the text is put back, and
a mark that disappeared while its quoted text is still there once is put back after it. A thread
deleted together with its mark counts as resolved.

## Comments and AI agents

Agents that edit the files see the marks right where they work, and the threads at the end of the
page. GitQuire keeps a section in the wiki's `AGENTS.md` that tells them the rules: read the
comments first, keep the marks when rewriting, reply with one line under the thread, never resolve
or edit someone else's comment. The rest of `AGENTS.md` belongs to your team; GitQuire only rewrites
the part between its `gitquire:begin` and `gitquire:end` markers. Set `GITQUIRE_AGENTS_FILE=off` to
stop GitQuire from writing it.

When a connected agent reads a page through GitQuire, it also gets the page's open threads with all
their comments, and listing a folder shows how many open threads each page has. So a comment like
"@claude please tighten this paragraph" is a request your agent sees the next time it works on the
page. A person resolves the thread once it's done.
